**The problems, in order of damage**

**1. The lead photograph is a dark corridor.**
The first image in the carousel — the one doing the most work — is a dim, orange-cast shot of an entrance passage. Nobody has ever looked at a corridor and decided to book dinner. The second slot is a 19-second video of table settings with someone's feet in the frame. The third is a long banquet table under a chandelier that reads as a function room rather than a restaurant.

There is no plated food, no warm full room, no exterior. For a charcoal-grill restaurant with a 58-year history, the visual story is currently: *narrow corridor, empty glassware, conference lunch.*

**2. The name doesn't match anywhere else.**
Google says **Armenian Taverna & Restaurant**. The trading entity is Armenian Taverna Limited. The new website brands it **ARMENIAN · BAR · RESTAURANT · LOUNGE**. Three different names.

Name, address and phone consistency across the web is a genuine local ranking factor. Pick the real-world name — whatever is actually on the signage — and make every surface identical.

**3. The address discrepancy is still unresolved.**
Princes Street vs Princess Street, M2 4DF vs M2 4DN. This is still outstanding from the run-up notes. It must be settled and then made byte-identical across the profile, the website footer, the schema markup and any directory listing.

**4. Nobody knows where the buttons go.**
- **Reserve a table** — if this points at a third-party platform, it should point at the NeroBooking widget (`https://eu.neropay.app/nerobooking/widget/24883`). Direct, no commission, and the bookings land in the system Arman already uses.
- **Order online** — if this is an aggregator, every order through it costs Arman 25–30%. If it's dead, it's worse. Check it.

**5. The 2026 AI summaries are being written from thin material.**
Google now generates place summaries from reviews, posts and web content. If the business description is short or generic and there are no posts, the summary is assembled from reviews alone. A properly written description plus a regular posting cadence gives Google better material to summarise — which increasingly determines what people see before they ever reach the profile.

*(Note: Google's 2026 content policy restricts AI-generated text in profiles. The description must be genuinely written. This aligns with the standing rule anyway.)*

**6. Almost certainly unused: posts, Q&A, attributes, secondary categories.**
None are visible. Each is free reach.

## CAN THE PHOTOS BE CHANGED, AND CAN YOU PICK WHAT SHOWS?

Short answer: **you can heavily influence it. You cannot control it.**

Here is the accurate picture:

- You **can** upload as many photos as you like, and set a **logo** and a **cover photo**.
- The cover photo is a **preference, not a setting.** Google states it may show a different image, and in practice it often does.
- What Google actually shows depends on **search query relevance** (someone searching "khachapuri" may get a khachapuri photo), **recency**, **image quality**, and its own algorithmic preferences.
- Google **auto-updates** the displayed image to recent uploads sometimes, regardless of what you set as cover.
- **Customer-uploaded photos also appear** in the carousel, and you cannot delete them. You can only report ones that breach policy. You can, however, drown them out by uploading enough good ones.

**What actually shifts the odds, per current guidance:**

| Tactic | Why |
|---|---|
| Upload at **1332 × 750** | Google's preferred cover ratio |
| Compose with the subject in the **top half** of the frame | Google crops from the top for some placements |
| Make sure it works cropped **square as well as landscape** | Different surfaces crop differently |
| Favour **exterior shots with an uncluttered background** for the cover | Algorithmically favoured |
| Upload **consistently over time**, not all at once | Recency is weighted |
| Remove weak owner-uploaded photos | Fewer bad candidates to choose from |

**So the honest answer to give Arman:** we can't guarantee a specific photo, but we can make sure that every photo Google *could* choose is a good one. That's the real goal — not controlling the pick, but removing all the bad options.

This is a direct instruction for the September shoot: **shoot a dedicated set at 1332×750 with the subject in the top half of the frame.** These are not the same crops as the website heroes.

**It isn't currently in it.** The retainer is £490/month — £40 website, £390 Meta bundle, £60 managed emails. Google Business Profile appears nowhere.

**Recommendation: fold it into what's already agreed. Do not add a line item.**

Three reasons:

1. **Arman hasn't confirmed the staged payments yet.** Adding a new charge before he's agreed the existing ones is the wrong move at the wrong moment.
2. **It's cheap to run.** Two hours to set up, then roughly twenty minutes a week — a fraction of what the Meta work costs to deliver.
3. **It produces the numbers that justify the retainer.** Calls and direction requests are the most commercially direct figures available, and they were already flagged as the strongest stats for the owner portal. When renewal comes up, "your direction requests are up 40%" is a far better argument than impressions on a Meta ad. Give it away now, get paid for it at renewal.

**Frame it to Arman as included in the £1,600 setup**, under the existing scope. Something like: *"Your Google listing is where most people actually find you, so it's part of the setup — I need manager access to work on it."* That also makes the access ask feel like a deliverable rather than a favour.

**If it ever becomes a line item**, it's £75–100/month standing alone. Don't sell it now. Hold it as a renewal lever.
